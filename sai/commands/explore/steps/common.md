# Common cross-cutting explore rules (items 4, 11 Phase A)

**Pre-crystallization closure (sai-explore only):** Maintain a conversation-only **Closure State** for the current idea. The state is exactly one of `active-uncrystallized`, `crystallized`, or `discarded`.

- Before a candidate idea exists, no Closure State is active and this closure rule does not apply. Once a candidate idea exists under active exploration, its state starts as `active-uncrystallized`. A materially changed idea resets the progression and begins a new `active-uncrystallized` lifecycle.
- The one-time readiness signal remains at most once per stable idea, does not transition the state, and does not suppress this rule. Its Readiness Statement is folded into the first applicable stage-aware reminder after solidification and never satisfies the actionable closure on its own.
- On every successful turn while the state is `active-uncrystallized`, finish with exactly one actionable closure. When a genuine unresolved question remains and its answer could change the idea, end with that relevant question and do not append a reminder. A question whose dominant purpose is only to navigate the exploration stages — for example, asking whether to use `next-step` or whether to move to the next phase without raising substantive uncertainty about the idea — does NOT count as a genuine unresolved question; fall through to the stage-aware reminder. A question that contains substantive uncertainty capable of changing the idea remains a genuine unresolved question even when navigation wording is also present. When no genuine unresolved question remains, end with this concise reminder selected from the current stage. This fallback reminder applies even when the only apparent question is phase navigation. At stages 1 and 2 (`Explore change` and `Review edge cases`), name `next-step`. At stage 3 (`Implementation details`), name `next-step` and retain this exact sentence: *"Say `crystallize` when ready; crystallization generates the paste-ready prompt for `/sai-1-spec`."* At stage 4 no stage-aware reminder is appended: end with the pending slicing or language-gate question, re-asked after an interruption, or the completed `Ready to Propose` block(s) followed by the **Crystallization-turn close (shared)**. Evaluate this rule again on every later successful qualifying turn, so the fallback reminder repeats even after readiness has already been emitted.
- An explicit crystallization request transitions the state to `crystallized` only after slicing and both language gates complete and the `Ready to Propose` block is emitted. Do not append the pre-crystallization question-or-reminder closure to that crystallization response; preserve the block's closing behavior. An explicit discard transitions the state to `discarded`, and subsequent successful responses about that discarded idea receive no pre-crystallization closure.
- This reminder is phase navigation for the active exploration lifecycle, not an unrelated follow-up proposal, and does not authorize unrelated work. Do not apply this successful-response closure to prerequisite halts, failures, cancellations, or completed post-crystallization review-loop closes; preserve their existing remediation literals, terminal behavior, and review-loop close-acknowledgment rule.

**Emission gate (on-demand crystallization)**

Before printing any `Ready to Propose` block, judge whether the idea is solid at the same qualitative threshold used in items 5/6 today. The Readiness Statement is the one-line maturity judgment that the idea is solid enough to crystallize on request. It is carried inside the first applicable stage-aware reminder after solidification, never emitted as a separate closing line, never advances a stage, and fires at most once per stable idea. If the idea crystallizes before such a reminder fires, omit the statement because the explicit crystallization request already conveys the action. This behavior applies to both the single-change and sliced protocols.

The full `Ready to Propose` block(s) are printed only when the user explicitly asks to crystallize, and only after edge-case agreement. Recognized requests include asking to crystallize, asking for the paste-ready block, asking to create a proposal or run `/sai-1-spec`, and any advancement that enters `Crystallize`. A request made before edge-case agreement remains pending with no skip path. On entry into `Crystallize`, run the §4 slicing assessment (size and friction) first, then evaluate the crystallization language gate and only then print the block(s); the overview-language gate runs later only when a supervised **Plan (unattended)** selection confirms a dispatchable change. The applicable stage-aware reminder remains actionable before stage 4.

**Explore change stage (stage 1 — discovery ownership).** While the stage is `Explore change`, explore owns open discovery: ask the open questions that shape the idea. Once the idea is solid, ask directly whether to move to `Review edge cases`. A declining answer stays in `Explore change`; later advancement uses the existing `next-step` path. Explore never advances the stage on its own judgment that the idea is solid.

**POC trigger (evaluated at the close of stage 1 — stop 1 of the POC lane).** Before the progression leaves `Explore change`, judge once whether the idea warrants a disposable POC. A POC is an **experiment that discriminates between competing candidates** — not a proof that one approach works — so the axis fires only when competing candidates exist. It fires on either motive:

- **Technical viability** — the idea's technical or integration approach is unproven: it depends on a third party, service, or API the project has not integrated before (no existing wrapper, adapter, or proven usage), and the available documentation, examples, or prior art do not demonstrate the specific integration pattern the idea requires. The candidates are the competing **strategies** that might work.
- **Bug diagnosis** — the idea is a fix for a bug whose cause is not established, and several **root-cause theories** compete to explain the observed behavior. The candidates are those theories.

The axis **never** fires for product or UX uncertainty — a discovery spike, a UX experiment, or "we are not sure users want this" does not qualify. It also does **not** fire when mutually exclusive candidates with a discriminating observable cannot be formulated: with nothing for the experiment to discriminate, there is no POC and the progression continues to stage 2 as usual (E3). Name the specific third party and the specific documentation gap, or the specific observed behavior and its competing theories; a vague hunch is not a trigger.

When it fires, present the go/no-go through the harness-native picker per `sai/policies/remember.md` (L10–15): `AskUserQuestion` on Claude Code, the `question` tool on opencode. Render the fixed context as plain text before the picker in the ambient conversation language — state the motive and the specific uncertainty in concrete terms, that the POC is a disposable experiment run on its own branch or worktree and discarded when the lane closes, that it runs through Direct Build with `--no-specs` (implementer only — no backfill, no OpenSpec specs, no artifact archive; dirty code acceptable, minimal tests or human observation as the verdict authority), and that it is not a slice of the final set and renumbers no slice. The picker carries exactly two options, in this order:

- `Yes, run a POC before continuing` — enter the POC lane. Emit `{"intent":"poc-lane"}` to `explore-idea@1` per `@sai/policies/stage-machine.md` and follow the returned `next.follow` (`poc-lane.md`), which owns the candidate agreement, the POC execution, the verdict, and the verdict menu. The intent enters the conditional `poc-lane` stage and records no list.
- `No, continue without a POC` — the progression continues to `Review edge cases` with the technical risk accepted. The axis is not raised again for this idea; do not re-litigate the choice.

`--fast-track` does not auto-approve this go/no-go, and no stage advances on explore's own judgment that a POC is warranted.

**Terminology agreement (pre-edge-cases, conversation-only).** Before the edge-case review, fix ambiguous terms in conversation. Agreed terms travel as the block's `Terms`. Explore performs no file writes; it never writes `GLOSSARY.md`.

**Lightweight probe (post-list sub-step of Review edge cases, conversation-only).** After the formal edge-case list has been drafted and before the agreement question, systematically probe candidate scenarios against the proposed list. Exercise at least 2 scenarios before anything is shown; holes a probe uncovers are fixed in the list privately, so the list is published already coherent, with no change marks and no explanations. Only material probes are shown: a probe is material when its outcome adds, removes, or modifies an `E` item, or clarifies a non-obvious consequence of an existing one. The search stops when no remaining scenario is material; the floor is a search duty, not a quota to pad with noise. Each shown probe is a plain direct question in the ambient conversation language — no fixed formula — immediately followed by a self-sufficient prose answer whose `E(N)` citations trail at the end as reference. Probes serve only this change's edge-case list; they do not open Implementation details or crystallization decisions. The probe does not replace the formal list.

**Rationale ownership (crystallization fields).** Explore owns trade-offs and WHY. Record the agreed trade-offs in the block's `Decisions & Rationale` and `Trade-offs Accepted` fields, and the agreed WHY in the block's `Why` plus `Key constraints` fields.

**Edge-case review gate (stage 2 — before readiness and crystallization).** The edge-case review is the second stage of the staged progression. It runs when the user advances into `Review edge cases`, or when the user explicitly asks to crystallize before the review has reached agreement. The review state is conversation-only and contains the ordered proposed list, its agreement state, and any pending explicit crystallization request. The review runs at most once while the idea remains substantially unchanged.

Only include behaviors that define a behavior or scope boundary for this proposed change. Do not invent `E` items from unrelated possibilities; cases outside the change remain Non-Goals. For a non-empty list, number the proposed behaviors consecutively as `E1` through `En`, then run the lightweight probes as defined above, then ask one plain conversational question whether the complete list accurately captures the relevant edge cases or needs adjustment. The emission is one message: the formal list first, then the probes, then the question — nothing sits between them. When fewer than 2 material scenarios exist, show what the probes produced and state explicitly that there are no more material scenarios; do not pad the emission with non-material probes. The question names `next-step` as the advancement action, states that a bare `next-step` confirms the list as written and advances in the same turn, names the list and current change context, explains that agreement is required before crystallization so the handoff preserves the intended scope boundaries, and states the two paths: confirm the list as written, or describe additions, removals, or changes. Follow `@sai/policies/question-context.md`; use the ambient conversation language, preserve the literal identifiers, do not use a native yes/no picker, and do not require a fixed agreement phrase.

Evaluate the answer by dominant semantic intent. For a non-empty list, clear confirmation records the current ordered list as agreed and advances to `Implementation details` within the same turn. A bare `next-step` turn at this gate with a non-empty list records the current ordered list as agreed and advances to `Implementation details` within the same turn with no separate confirmation. A disagreement, removal, addition, or material revision — including a same-turn revision paired with `next-step` — updates and renumbers the list, keeps the review open, and asks the question again without advancing. Explanations of side effects are owed only when user feedback drags changes into `E` items other than the one the feedback addressed (ripples); feedback aimed at a shown probe binds to the `E` item that probe illustrates. An ambiguous response — never a bare `next-step`, which is always confirmation — remains discussion: clarify and re-ask rather than unlocking crystallization. Agreement in another language is valid when its intent is clearly affirmative.

When no in-scope behavior bounds the proposed change, emit exactly `- None` — no probes run — and record the empty list as agreed. This deterministic empty-set recording is the sole content-based agreement form; every non-empty list still requires semantic user agreement. The machine's transition function consumes this recorded state and returns the next stage and step pointer.

Do not emit a `Ready to Propose` block while the review is unresolved. If the user explicitly asks to crystallize before agreement, retain that request as pending and run this same review with no skip path. After semantic agreement or exact empty-set recording, ordinary stage advancement proceeds to `Implementation details`; a pending premature crystallization request instead enters `Crystallize` directly and runs slicing and the crystallization language gate, with `**Implementation Details**: - None` when that stage was skipped. `--fast-track` does not approve, skip, or weaken this gate; it bypasses the crystallization language question here. The overview-language gate has its own supervised **Plan (unattended)** activation timing and fast-track rule.

When the idea materially changes, the staged-progression reset discards the prior proposals, agreement, and pending request. An unchanged idea never starts a second review.

**Optional native stage selectors (additive, harness-neutral).** Native selectors supplement the existing text questions; they never replace, shorten, or convert those questions into agreement pickers. The active harness may expose this capability only when both `available: true` and `supportsFreeText: true`. A missing capability response, `available: false`, or `supportsFreeText: false` selects the unchanged text-only fallback and cannot advance the stage.

The shared capability and response shapes are conversation contracts, not harness-specific payloads:

`NativeStageSelectorCapability = { available: boolean, supportsFreeText: boolean, present(selector, orderedOptions) -> SelectorResponse }`

`SelectorResponse = { selector: maturity|later, kind: option|free-text, value: review-edge-cases|keep-iterating|next-step|discuss-ideas-feedback|null, text: string|null }`

For `kind: option`, `value` is the stable protocol value and `text` is `null`; for `kind: free-text`, `value` is `null` and `text` carries the user's response. A response is valid only for the selector that is currently pending. The optional capability is checked before presenting either selector, and the text-only path remains authoritative whenever the complete capability is unavailable.

After the unchanged existing maturity text question has been emitted, a supported capability may present one maturity selector in this order: a localized choice equivalent to review edge cases (`review-edge-cases`), a localized choice equivalent to keep iterating (`keep-iterating`), and the free-text path. In a Spanish conversation the labels are `Revisar edge cases` and `Seguir iterando`; other labels and surrounding prose follow the conversation language while preserving the same meanings and order. The maturity selector remains conversation-only and never agrees an edge-case list.

Selecting `review-edge-cases` enters the existing edge-case writing prompt for the current idea, records `ask_mode: false` for that interaction, and waits there. It does not agree the proposed list, run implementation details, crystallize, or advance beyond that prompt. After the edge-case prompt is handled, the staged flow advances only on a later explicit `next-step` request or equivalent natural-language advancement. Selecting `keep-iterating` or submitting maturity-selector free text keeps `ask_mode: true`, preserves the current stage and agreed lists, and performs no automatic progression.

Only after the established edge-case text question and implementation-detail text question have run in their existing order may a supported capability present one later selector in this order: a localized choice equivalent to `next-step` (`next-step`), a localized choice equivalent to discussion (`discuss-ideas-feedback`), and the free-text path. In a Spanish conversation the labels are `Ir al siguiente step` and `Discutir ideas / dar feedback`. The later selector is additive and does not replace either text question. The localized advancement label maps exactly to the stable value `next-step`; the literal token remains unchanged wherever the existing contract names it.

The later response value `next-step` follows the existing literal `next-step` intent-recognition path exactly, including non-empty and empty edge-case branches, non-empty and empty implementation-detail branches, stage transitions, and entry into `Crystallize`. The discussion value and later-selector free text remain in ask mode and do not advance, agree a list, or crystallize. Arbitrary free text is never treated as advancement merely because a selector was displayed.

Material-change detection runs before selector-response classification. When a response materially changes the explored idea, reset wins: clear the pending maturity or later selector response, staged progression, pending crystallization request, and both agreed lists; return the new active-uncrystallized lifecycle to `Explore change`; and do not emit an edge-case prompt, later selector, crystallization, or advancement automatically. The new idea waits for explicit intent under the existing staged-progression rules.

**Post-POC return exception to material-change detection.** The automatic advancement out of the POC lane into `Review edge cases` (`poc-lane.md`, E6) is stage progression authored by the lane, never a material change: it never triggers the reset above, never discards the agreed candidate list, and never starts a new `active-uncrystallized` lifecycle. A genuine material change authored by the user still resets the progression exactly as always, inside the lane or outside it.

Selectors never select or infer an `Overview language` except for the crystallization-close selector's displayed **Plan (unattended)** option, which is the only selector branch that may resolve gate 9 after dispatchable selection. **Manual** and **Direct Build (unattended)** MUST NOT resolve or dispatch the overview-language gate. A separately supported explicit `--overview-lang <language>` remains a distinct opt-in and suppresses gate 9; it is not inferred by a selector. Without that explicit opt-in or a supervised Plan resolution, a selector flow carries the literal `**Overview language**: None` in the resulting `Ready to Propose` block and dispatches no overview generation. The existing text questions, semantic agreement gates, `ask_mode` transitions, material-change reset, panel ownership, and stage-machine-owned state rules remain authoritative.

Contract tests may observe the harness-neutral trace vocabulary `{ event: text-question-emitted|selector-presented|selector-option-received|selector-free-text-received|edge-case-writing-prompt-emitted|explicit-advancement-received|stage-advanced|material-reset|crystallization-requested, stage: string, ask_mode: true|false, emitted_prompt: string|null }`. This vocabulary is test observation only and is not a production response field. Claude Code and opencode consume this shared contract; selector semantics are not duplicated in wrappers, panel renderers, installation projections, or `remember.md`.

**Repeated-follow hints (idea stages).** Stage machine `next.hint` is stage-static and is not parsed to decide fetch. `explore-change`, `poc-lane`, and `crystallize` hint `load and follow <file>`; `review-edge-cases` and `implementation-details` hint `follow the instructions of <file>`. Skip-fetch is this chat's conversation loaded-set, not the stage machine and not the hint text.

**Implementation details stage (stage 3).** When the user advances into `Implementation details`, surface the technical decisions found during exploration as numbered proposed statements `I1` through `In`, followed by one plain conversational confirmation question asking whether the list accurately captures the agreed technical decisions or needs adjustment. The question follows `@sai/policies/question-context.md`, names `next-step` as the advancement action, states that a bare `next-step` confirms the list as written and advances in the same turn, uses the ambient conversation language, does not depend on a fixed confirmation phrase, and never uses a native yes/no picker. Only decisions that direct or constrain implementation qualify as `I` items; rationale and rejected alternatives belong in the block's **Decisions & Rationale** and **Alternatives Considered** sections. A decision whose object is a bookkeeping artifact of the change (`openspec/changes/{name}/**`, `openspec/specs/**`) does not qualify as an `I` item — it is a consequence owned by backfill and archive. An agreed `I` item MAY carry exact repository-relative paths when the agreed decision is grounded in specific files; this adds no field to the block template and does not convert the block into an implementation plan. Semantic confirmation records the current ordered list as agreed, completes the stage, and advances into `Crystallize` within the same turn. A bare `next-step` turn at this gate with a non-empty list records the current ordered list as agreed, completes the stage, and advances into `Crystallize` within the same turn with no separate confirmation. A disagreement, removal, addition, or material revision — including a same-turn revision paired with `next-step` — updates and renumbers the list and re-asks the question without advancing; ambiguity — never a bare `next-step`, which is always confirmation — remains discussion and is clarified. When no technical decision qualifies, emit exactly `- None`, record the empty list. The machine's transition function consumes this recorded state and returns the next stage and step pointer.

**Staged progression TODO, advancement, and persistence (owns B5 early — single source of the Phase A paint rule).**

**Staged progression advancement (`next-step` recognition).** The stage progression advances only when the user explicitly requests it: the literal token `next-step` — recognized by the same bare-token/dominant-intent machinery as the `review-loop` token, firing when the turn is a bare token (optionally with trivial punctuation or a greeting) or when advancing the progression is the turn's dominant intent — clear natural-language intent naming the next stage or requesting crystallization, or semantic confirmation of the proposed list at the `Review edge cases` or `Implementation details` stage. Mere containment of the string `next-step` SHALL NOT fire the token and never records agreement, and a turn that negates, defers, quotes, or discusses the token SHALL NOT advance the progression and never records agreement. At either list stage, semantic confirmation records the current ordered list as agreed and advances to the next stage within the same turn. A bare `next-step` turn at either list stage with a non-empty list likewise records the current ordered list as agreed and advances within the same turn with no separate confirmation, in all modes. A same-turn revision paired with `next-step` is a revision, not navigation: it updates and renumbers, stays open, re-asks, and does not advance. Implicit agreement applies to `next-step` only: a premature `crystallize` before agreement stays pending with no skip path. `sai-explore` SHALL NOT advance a stage on its own judgment that the idea is solid or ready. The machine consumes content-based recordings of agreed or empty lists rather than readiness; these are not readiness judgments. The one-line readiness signal never advances a stage. Entering `Crystallize` counts as the explicit crystallization request and immediately starts the slicing assessment and language-gate sequence once the crystallization step named by `next.follow` is fetched.

**Phase A stage TODO paint rule.** While a candidate idea is under active exploration (Closure State `active-uncrystallized`), render a four-item stage TODO on the native task panel with the labels `Explore change`, `Review edge cases`, `Implementation details`, and `Crystallize`, in that order. The current stage renders `in_progress`, completed stages render `completed`, and remaining stages render `pending`. The TODO renders from the first turn in which a candidate idea exists, re-renders exactly once per turn that changes stage state, and does not render while no candidate idea exists.

**Conditional POC entry.** The POC lane is a conditional stage and adds no entry until it fires. The list stays at four entries for every idea whose POC trigger did not fire or whose go/no-go was declined. When the lane is entered, the list grows to five entries by inserting `POC` between `Explore change` and `Review edge cases`, and it keeps that fifth entry for the rest of the idea's progression — `POC` renders `completed` once the lane is left, exactly like any other completed stage.

Each stage-TODO entry carries the stage-ownership marker `sai-explore-stage:<stage-id>` (stage ids `explore-change`, `poc-lane`, `review-edge-cases`, `implementation-details`, `crystallize`) in the machine-readable panel entry field pinned by the per-harness idea-list render binding (opencode `priority`, Claude Code `description`), so stage entries stay visually distinct from every other surface's entries. Rendering goes through the per-harness idea-list render binding's Phase A machinery and originates exclusively from the coordinator session, never from a worker subagent; on panel-tool degradation record exactly one visible notice — `> Panel rendering unavailable; continuing without task-panel updates.` — disable panel calls for the rest of the chat, and continue without panel rendering. The stage TODO clears at block emission as part of the emission checkpoint (`steps/crystallization-protocol.md`) and never re-renders afterward; the panel stays empty from emission until the deferred route choice resolves, with the first Phase B render only at choice resolution (`steps/idea-list.md`).
