## Why

The repeated `ok` lines that cache warming produced in the main session read as answers to the user and confused them. The subagent `ok` carried no meaning either, and after a dispose it hid that warming had ended. Every warming acknowledgement now states what happened.

## What Changes

- `sai/policies/cache-warming.md` defines three reply literals once in its Terms: the wake literal `warming context cache up` (subagent reply to a `continue` or `finished` wake), the stop literal `cache warming stopped` (subagent reply to `dispose`, to every wake after it, and to a wake that is not `wait.js` JSON), and the main-session literal `➡️ Warming context cache up.` (main-session turn opened only by warming, with nothing to handle). It also defines normalized recognition (letter case, surrounding whitespace, one trailing period) and keeps a bare `ok` as a legacy alias.
- The wake, repeat, hand-off, error-wake, and `dispose` rules of `cache-warming.md` use the literals instead of `ok`. A `finished` wake keeps the wake literal, because a superseded wait also returns `finished` while the cache is still warm. The repeat of the last result is unchanged.
- § Wake acknowledgement recognizes the wake literal, the stop literal, and `ok` by normalized recognition; any other text (a translation, a paraphrase, a literal with words added) is neither an acknowledgement nor a result. The stop literal never decides whether an agent has ended. Recognition stays an instruction rule; no tool parses the literals.
- A new **Fixed text** paragraph makes the literals fixed English protocol text under `public-chat.md` § Protocol preservation; only the main-session literal carries the `➡️` prefix. `budget-executor`, `/sai-cache-warmer`, and `/sai-context-cache-ttl-benchmark` keep their own replies.
- `sai/policies/worker-lifecycle.md` (§ Stop call, § Worker side) and `sai/policies/worker-report-transport.md` (schema exclusions, § Undelivered reports) name the literals by their term and point to `cache-warming.md` instead of spelling `ok`.
- `CONTINUATION_REMINDER` in `skills/universal/sai-cache-warmer/wait.js` names the main-session literal and the subagent wake and stop literals. No other `wait.js` behavior changes.
- `sai/policies/change-telemetry.md`, `docs/commands/sai-retrospective.md`, and `docs/project-settings.md` replace their mentions of the wake and dispose `ok`; a dispose reply is still never counted as a wake. `sai/tools/claude-transcripts.js` and `sai/tools/opencode-db.js` change only comments; wake detection still comes from the wait command.
- `AGENTS.md` describes the repeat of the last result as replacing the wake literal.
- Tests cover the three literals, the alias, the normalization rule, the reminder text, and the updated policy wording.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None

### Modified Capabilities

- `agent-self-warming`: wake replies of subagents and of the main session become explicit literals, defined once with normalized recognition and the legacy `ok` alias; the continuation reminder names them.
- `worker-lifecycle-protocol`: the worker-side reply to `dispose` and to later wakes is the stop literal, named by pointer to `cache-warming.md`.
- `worker-dispose`: the dispose scenarios reply with the stop literal instead of `ok`.
- `subagent-warming-declaration`: the real-message definition excludes wake acknowledgements by their literals instead of `ok`.
- `undelivered-report-recovery`: acknowledgement handling and the not-a-result rule use the literals and the alias.
- `explore-handoff-warming`: a hand-off wake with nothing to handle prints the main-session literal.
- `change-telemetry`: the wake definition and wake-cost wording name the reply literals; detection stays on the wait command.
