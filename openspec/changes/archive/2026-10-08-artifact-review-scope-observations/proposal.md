## Why

In an analyzed Plan - Unattended run, the design review raised a High finding that no production path wrote a new column, although the approved specs required no write path. The design worker turned that finding into a scope question, and the question stopped the route for about ten minutes. The artifact review contract gave such an observation no channel other than a High finding: its High definition counted "incomplete" without reference to what the change was asked to deliver.

## What Changes

- `sai/policies/artifact-review-contract.md` gains a `## Scope observations` section. It defines the approved scope of each reviewed artifact set and defines a scope observation as a true observation whose fix requires behavior the approved scope does not ask for. Every observation is classified against the approved scope before a severity is assigned.
- A scope observation carries no severity and no identifier. It stays outside the findings block and outside the `Summary:` counts, so it extends no review round, hands the worker nothing to correct, and raises no user question.
- The `High` definition states that "incomplete" is measured against the approved scope. An artifact that omits or contradicts something the approved scope asks for, or that adds behavior the approved scope does not ask for, stays a finding.
- The surface that ran the review lists scope observations in its final report under the label `Scope observations`, one line each in the form `- <observation> — consequence: <what stays true of the change without the unrequested behavior>`, and prints no such section when there is none.
- `sai/commands/explore/steps/pipeline-plan-unattended.md`, `sai/commands/explore/steps/review-loop.md`, and `sai/commands/explore/steps/pipeline-direct-build.md` point to that section without restating the definition. The Plan - Unattended final report also carries the scope observations named in the design worker's terminal summary. The Direct Build - Unattended terminal report gains a `Scope observations` record.
- The contract also defines the approved scope of a Direct Build diff or draft as what its `Ready to Propose` block asks for, and Direct Build classifies the observations of its functional fix loop and its spec review by the same section.
- `sai/commands/design/steps/design.md`, under "Spec-problem handling during design", classifies a discovered problem first. A scope observation is recorded in `design.md` as a non-goal in the form "out of scope: X — revisit when Y", is named in the worker's terminal summary, and raises no question, in every run. Every other problem is a spec defect and keeps the existing clarity path.
- The severity vocabulary, the five-field finding shape, the identifier scheme, and the `Summary:` tally form are unchanged. The design phase contract gains no scope observation field.
- The `sai/policies/` row of `AGENTS.md` names the scope observation rule.
- A new test file pins these texts.

Known limitations:

- A route can complete the artifacts of a change that stays inert without the unrequested behavior. The user learns this in the final report, after the design work is done.
- The classification is a judgment of the reviewer against the approved scope.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

None.

### Modified Capabilities

- `review-finding-format`: the shared contract separates scope observations from findings, measures High incompleteness against the approved scope, and defines the report line form.
- `design-planning-worker`: the design worker classifies a discovered spec problem against the approved scope before the clarity path and records a scope observation as a non-goal with no question.
- `supervised-review-reporting`: the Plan - Unattended final report prints the scope observations of the review rounds and of the design worker.
- `explore-pipeline-supervision`: Direct Build - Unattended classifies review observations by the shared contract and reports scope observations in its terminal report.
