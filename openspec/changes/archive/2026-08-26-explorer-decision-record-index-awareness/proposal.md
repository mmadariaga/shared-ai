## Why

The two decision-record indexes (ADR and DDR) are the only place in a project that records which decision currently governs an area and which prior decisions have been superseded — critical metadata that no text search can infer. Currently, every reference to these indexes within the repository is a writer or maintainer operation (`sai/commands/implement/steps/artifact-analysis.md`, `sai/commands/retire-docs/body.md`); no research surface consumes them. An explorer answering "why is this the way it is" reads current text that looks equally authoritative whether the decision behind it stands or was reversed. By documenting the indexes as available research inputs with enough structural detail for self-service consultation, the explorer can make its own judgment about relevance and access this critical decision context.

## What Changes

A new `## Decision-record index` section is added to `sai/policies/explore-agent.md` between `## Ladder precedence` and `## Filesystem research scope`. The section describes:

- The two index locations (`docs/adr/0000-INDEX.md` and `docs/ddr/0000-INDEX.md`) and their optional nature
- The canonical five-section skeleton: Conventions, By <domain unit>, Cross-cutting categories, extension/correction table, and Superseded (historical)
- The closed set of relationship tokens (Pair with, Refs, Amends, Reframes, Reverses, Supersedes) with cross-family identifier encoding (adr:NNNN / ddr:NNNN)
- The current vs. historical separation strategy that distinguishes in-force decisions from superseded ones
- The relevance judgment principle: the explorer determines whether an index serves the task, and this determination is not recorded

Test coverage is added to `test/explorer-prompt-discipline.test.js` to verify the policy section's presence and structure.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

**explorer-index-research** — The explorer has access to a complete description of the ADR and DDR index structure, their relationship-token vocabulary, and the distinction between current and historical entries. This enables the explorer to independently consult decision-record indexes when investigating the rationale, governance, or history of a decision, without requiring the decision metadata to be embedded in textual sources.
