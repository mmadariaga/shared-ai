## Why

During a rebase, `/sai-merge`'s containment facts compared the replayed commit against `base..HEAD`, which includes the rewritten copies of the branch's own earlier commits, so a repeated subject such as "fix tests" could look like evidence that a change was already contained. Every conflict stop also recomputed the expensive upstream history (patch ids, commit lists) with no cache (issue #486, F7 and F8).

## What Changes

- During a rebase, `merge.js` reads `onto` and the original head from the rebase state git keeps (`rebase-merge/`, or `rebase-apply/`) and uses `base..onto` as the upstream range, so the branch's already replayed commits are never upstream evidence. A merge or a cherry-pick keeps `base..HEAD`, and a rebase state without `onto` falls back to it. The stop reports the `upstream_tip`.
- During a rebase, the history facts of the upstream range (its commits, their patch ids, and the upstream commits that touch each conflicted file) are computed once and kept in `history-facts.json` in the directory of the `--record` file, the merge run directory, keyed by the `onto` and original-head SHAs and the range. The stop reports `history_reused`. A missing, unreadable, or differently keyed cache recomputes the facts, and a cache that cannot be read or written never fails the conflict capture.
